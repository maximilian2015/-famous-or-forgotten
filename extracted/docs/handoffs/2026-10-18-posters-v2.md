# Posters V2

18 Oct 2026, branch `posters-v2`. The poster system was rebuilt because the filmography felt like
one poster repeated.

## Why they repeated (the audit)

- **One layout.** Every sheet was a colour field, a backdrop across the bottom, a subject in the
  middle and the title at the foot. The tens of thousands of layer combinations (backdrop ×
  subject × light × weather × scheme) all sat in that one layout. At 44px the title is hidden,
  so every thumbnail read as "a coloured field with a shape in the middle".
- **One body.** Eight of the eighteen subjects were the same glyph (a round head on a rounded
  torso) drawn alone, in pairs, in threes and in fives. That is 83% of comedy and musical
  subjects, 71% of crime and 57% of drama, romance and sci-fi.
- **Two inputs.** The generator knew the title and the genre. It did not know the scale, the
  format or the part, so a tentpole looked like an indie.
- **No family.** The seed was the full title, so "Light and Rain III" was unrelated to "Light
  and Rain".

## How it works now

`poster-spec.js posterPlan({ title, genre, type, scale })` is plain JS and tested. It returns:

- **root / part / season**, parsed from the title (` II`…` VI`, `· Part N`, `· season N`);
- **format**: film, series, soap, ad or festival (read from the type, then the scale);
- **tier**: small, studio or event (from the scale, then the type);
- **composition**, one of 14. Each has its own structure and its own place for the title:
  hero, faceoff, ensemble, symbol, vista, closeup, corridor, split, stage, skyline, action,
  type, panels, product. The pick is weighted by:
  - genre (`GENRE_T`: horror is an object, a corridor or a face; romance is two people or a
    horizon; a musical is a stage);
  - tier (`TIER_T`: an indie sells a mood or an object, a tentpole sells spectacle);
  - format (`FORMAT_T`: a series and a soap add cast panels; an advert is only the product);
- **motif**: one bold object per genre (`MOTIF`), or a product for adverts;
- **palette**: the existing schemes, unchanged.

**Franchises.** Everything is seeded by the franchise root, so every part shares palette,
composition and motif:

- **Part II** is mirrored, cropped 1.22× closer, its hue turned 40°, and its light (the accent
  that shows on a dark sheet) taken from another turn of the family.
- **Part III** escalates to the next composition up (`ESCALATE`).
- **Every sequel** carries its Roman numeral, which is still readable in a thumbnail.

**Marks:**

| sheet | mark |
|---|---|
| tentpole | a frame round the whole sheet, and a larger title |
| festival | a laurel wreath with OFFICIAL SELECTION, on the side away from the title |
| series | the band A SERIES |
| soap | the band WEEKNIGHTS AT SEVEN |

**Titles** are fitted by width (`fitTitle`; `stackTitle` for the typographic sheet). There is no
fixed length cut-off, which used to run "LIGHT AND RAIN" off both edges.

`Poster.jsx` draws the plan. The old backdrop, light and weather layers are reused inside the
compositions. Every call site now passes `scale`.

Old saves: posters are derived from each credit's title, genre, type and scale, so nothing is
stored and nothing migrates.

## Judging a poster change

```
cd extracted
node tools/poster-sheet.mjs ../saves/famous-Alex-Moon-53-2079.json out.html "label"
```

This draws every credit in a save through the real `Poster`. You get:

- a strip of 46px thumbnails, newest first, as in the filmography;
- large posters grouped as comedy, horror, blockbuster, indie, festival, series, soap and
  commercial;
- each franchise as I / II / III (part III is added by name when the save has none).

Run it before and after a change. `tests/test_posters.mjs` holds the structural checks:

- any ten in a row use five or more compositions;
- every genre reaches five or more compositions;
- the genre shares above hold;
- tentpole and indie differ;
- franchise parts are family but not clones;
- an advert is the product and a soap is its cast;
- titles fit.

The brightness checks from the first version are kept.
