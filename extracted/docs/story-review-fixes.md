# Story generator — the review's six cases, and how each class of mistake is now caught

Branch story-v1. Not in main.

## The six cases

**Was:** the title promised music; the story was a car accident.

### Music Inside Your Heart (2051)

- **Old:** A boarding school in the fog, a confession on tape, and it is your character who started it.
- **New:** A sound engineer is in a courthouse in a town of nine hundred people when a recording of the key witness has an echo that should not be there. The deadline is not the real deadline.
- **Hidden twist:** The echo places the recording in a room that was demolished before the crime.
- **Your part (not the lead):** the source who stops answering

**Was:** no wedding in the story.

### Small Wedding (2076)

- **Old:** A city under snow that will not stop, a disappearance everyone agreed to forget, and the town has already decided who did it.
- **New:** A teacher who wrote letters he never posted comes home for a small wedding he cannot get out of. Then the one person he never got over turns out to be the best man, and neither of them is free to say so.
- **Hidden twist:** The bride arranged the whole wedding to put them in one room.

**Was:** the hero was a man and his copy; no woman anywhere.

### The Last Woman (2076)

- **Old:** A hotel kept open for one guest, a disappearance everyone agreed to forget, and the only witness is nine years old.
- **New:** The engineer who checks every number twice is alone on a station that has stopped hearing from anybody when a woman’s voice answers on a channel nobody has used in years. The decision has to be made by people who will not live with it.
- **Hidden twist:** The voice is the last woman who served here. She never left, and she has been keeping him alive.

**Was:** the hero and the player’s part were both a juror.

### Ashes and Angels (2052)

- **Old:** An island with one ferry a day, a diagnosis kept from the family, and neither of them will say the thing out loud.
- **New:** A juror who just wants to go home is caught up in the trial of a teenager for a fire that killed two. Then a stranger hands her a note outside the court, and the truth would end her career.
- **Hidden twist:** The stranger is the defendant’s mother, and the note is her own confession.
- **Your part (not the lead):** the foreman who wants it over by lunch

**Was:** the heroine came home twice in one line.

### A Voice Above Your Door (2075)

- **Old:** A hotel kept open for one guest, a photograph that should not exist, and the money ran out in the first week.
- **New:** A daughter who stayed away too long comes back to the flat above the family shop, shut up in the spring. A voice on an old cassette says something nobody was meant to hear, and nobody in the room will say the thing out loud.
- **Hidden twist:** The voice on the tape is her own, aged six, repeating what she heard through the floor.
- **Your part (not the lead):** the neighbour who knew everything

**Was:** part two jumped to a border crossing with “two hours before anybody can get off”.

### Radiant Signal → Radiant Signal II

- **Old, part 1:** A block of flats due for demolition, a wedding that has to happen on Friday, and the town has already decided who did it.
- **Old, part 2:** An island with one ferry a day, a disappearance everyone agreed to forget, and the two of them have not spoken in twenty years.
- **New, part 1:** A ship’s radio officer is on an island with one ferry a day when a distress signal comes in from a boat that sank twenty years ago. The person asking already knows.
- **Part 1 twist:** The signal is coming from close by, on a channel only the crew should know.
- **New, part 2:** Two years later, a ship’s radio officer is still living with what came out — the signal is coming from close by, on a channel only the crew should know. Then a woman everyone agreed had disappeared is on the passenger list, and there are two hours left before anybody can get off.
- **Part 2 twist:** She never left; the woman who did was somebody else.

## What now catches each class of mistake (not a list of banned words)

| Check | What it reads | Found and fixed |
|---|---|---|
| **Title ↔ story** | The word a title is *about* (its head: "Wedding" in "Small Wedding") must be answered by the story. Every word the title generator uses now has a meaning (water, home, love, guilt…). The head word outranks the player's memory; adjectives do not. | Old titles: head word answered ~80–95% (was ~50–70%). New films get a title **from** the story: 97–100%. |
| **Player's part ≠ hero** | A role never has the hero's head word; a role whose wording has a gender only goes to a player of that gender. | 22 gendered roles had no gender — a man could be handed "the dancer who keeps a gun in her shoe". |
| **Place, action, twist** | Every "the X" in the incident, the obstacle and the twist must be brought in by the story, or be something the shape's world always has (a film crew has a director). Otherwise the part must say what it needs. | "The judges" at a tea dance, a recording "of the band" in a theatre with no band, a "rival's grandchild" with no rival, "the line" with no train, a miner's wife in a fishing port. |
| **Same action twice** | The hero is not described doing what the template then has them do — compared by word roots ("came home" / "comes back"). | 16 cases. |
| **Genre** | No part is shared by two genres, so no story borrows half of another genre. | 2 cases. |
| **Sequels** | Every part two of every part one a shape can make (17 155): the same hero by name, part one's twist said out loud, a new incident, nothing part one's place could not have, every "the X" known from part one, the sentence rules. Four different openings; the last one used is not used again. | The border-crossing case, and its cause. |
| **Sentences** | Every combination (10 222), for a hero and a heroine: unfilled words, "he have", "her her", plural/singular, a word twice in the logline. | 0. |

Each checker was tested by breaking the generator on purpose and seeing it fail.

## New films: the story first, then its title

- **Broken Weekend** (Comedy) — A high-court judge has to spend a week as the entertainer at a children’s holiday camp. Then it turns out she is very, very good at it, and the rival camp cheats openly and wins everything.
  *Twist:* She was good at it once before, as a child, and gave it up on purpose.
- **The Ninth Kiss** (Romance) — A chef three weeks from losing his star takes over the family bakery for a month while his father recovers. Then the one person he never got over turns out to live next door, and one of them has already decided to leave again.
  *Twist:* They moved there years ago, in case he ever came back.
- **The Ballroom of Glass** (Musical) — A widower who promised his wife he would learn the waltz has eight weeks to get ready for the regional championship. Then the partner breaks an ankle in week three, and the only music that works is a song nobody can stand.
  *Twist:* The ankle is fine. The partner faked it so he would have to dance with the person he actually loves.
- **The Second Country** (Drama) — A miner’s wife who has never made a speech holds on in a mining town in the eleventh month of a strike. Then one man goes back to work, and the town is splitting down the middle.
  *Twist:* He went back to pay for his wife’s operation, and the union quietly paid for it.
- **The Second Hunger** (Horror) — A census taker arrives at a village that goes hungry every year in the same week of July. A name is carved high in the church where nobody could reach, and the only road out floods at the same time every year.
  *Twist:* The name is his own, and the carving is two hundred years old.
- **Last Machine** (Thriller) — An engineer who maintains the cables works nights at a listening station on the coast. Then a colleague vanishes the morning after showing him something, and every call he makes is being heard.
  *Twist:* The colleague vanished on purpose, and left him the key.
- **The Second Witness** (Crime) — An internal affairs officer nobody talks to is asked to find a leak in the drug squad. Then a dead informant’s phone has her number in it, and the only witness is the one most likely to disappear.
  *Twist:* The informant was her own father, working both sides for years.
- **The City of Debts** (Sci-Fi) — A clerk who files what other people would rather forget lives in a city where a clinic will remove any painful year. Then a client asks to forget him, and getting it back means losing what he has now.
  *Twist:* The client is his own partner, and the two of them have done this four times.
- **The City of Light** (Comedy) — Six pensioners from the church choir decide to rob back the pension fund their old boss emptied. Then a real gang turns up the same night with the same idea, and the policeman who lives next door keeps popping round.
  *Twist:* The real gang are their own grandchildren, who thought of it first.
- **Names and Rain** (Romance) — A nineteen-year-old with one season left before university spends a summer running a beach café that will be gone by October. Then a storm closes everything for three days, and it ends on the first of September whatever happens.
  *Twist:* The storm was forecast a week ahead, and the other one stayed anyway.
- **Careless Encore** (Musical) — A composer stuck on the same page for ten years has one season left with the city orchestra. Then a child prodigy is sent to be his pupil, and the premiere is booked and sold out.
  *Twist:* The child is his own grandchild, and nobody ever told him.
- **Blue House** (Drama) — A nurse who has not been home since the funeral comes back to a house four siblings grew up in and nobody can afford to keep. A photograph shows their mother with a man none of them knows, and it is too late to be fair about it.
  *Twist:* The man is the father of only one of them.
- **Mercies and Blood** (Horror) — A vet just out of training is working in a valley where the cattle are dying one by one. Then a man is found with teeth marks no animal makes, and the farmers would rather shoot first.
  *Twist:* The teeth marks are human, and they match her own.
- **Nothing Behind Your Window** (Thriller) — A surgeon on the way home from a double shift hits somebody on an empty road at night and drives on. Then somebody leaves a photograph of the car under his windscreen wiper, and the police get closer every day.
  *Twist:* The photograph was taken by the person he hit, who lived.
- **Neon City** (Crime) — A boxer at the end of his career gets by in a city where two gangs share the same streets. Then the boss is shot and nobody knows who did it, and getting out costs more than getting in.
  *Twist:* The boss staged it, to see who would move first.
- **Zenith** (Sci-Fi) — A shuttle pilot is on a planet with three months left before its sun swells. Then the ground starts answering through the instruments, and the people who decide have already left.
  *Twist:* The planet has been alive all along, and it is frightened too.

## In the game (branch story-v1, stage 1 only)

A new first film in one of the eight genres gets its story once, when the offer is dressed. The card shows two sentences (the synopsis) and who you would play — the hero, or one of the story's other people for a supporting part. The twist is kept on the film and shown nowhere. The story travels with the project to the credit. Offers already in an old save, films already made, sequels, seasons and series are not touched.

## Still open

- **Tension before the reveal.** A twist should be hinted in the synopsis ("the voice on it sounds familiar"). That is a hint per twist — about 250 lines of writing.
- **Old titles** whose head word has no part in that genre (smoke in a comedy) stay loosely linked. More parts would fix it, and no new shapes are being added now.
- **Stage 1b**, titles from the story inside the game: needs an audit of everything that reads an offer's title first.
